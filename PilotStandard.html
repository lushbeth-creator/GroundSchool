How we fly matters.

Quota tells you what success produces. The Pilot Standard breaks down the repeatable behaviors that create it. Select each principle to explore what it looks like in the field, what strong execution sounds like and how you can apply it during your first 90 days.

01

Own Your Runway

Run your territory like a business.

What it means

Take responsibility for your activity, pipeline and results. Plan your week intentionally and know where you stand before someone has to ask.

What it looks like

  • Prospecting is scheduled, not squeezed in.
  • Leads and activity are entered in Stratus.
  • Your pipeline reflects what is actually happening.
  • You know the next action on active opportunities.

Ask yourself

"If my leader opened Stratus today, would it tell the same story I would?"

02

Lead With Curiosity

Diagnose before you prescribe.

What it means

Qualification is not a checklist you rush through. Learn how the merchant operates, what they use today, where friction exists and what matters to them.

What it looks like

  • Ask about the business before talking product.
  • Understand volume, ticket, card mix and environment.
  • Identify operational and technology needs.
  • Know when to involve Product or Solutions Engineering.

Ask yourself

"Do I know enough to explain why this solution fits this merchant?"

03

Move With Purpose

Turn activity into forward motion.

What it means

Being busy is not the same as building a business. Each conversation, follow-up and CRM update should move the opportunity toward a clear next step.

What it looks like

  • Follow-up has a reason and a date.
  • Merchant commitments are documented.
  • Applications are complete before handoff.
  • You remove avoidable delays before submission.

Ask yourself

"What specifically needs to happen next, and who owns it?"

04

Protect the Relationship

Sell for the relationship you want to keep.

What it means

Set accurate expectations, solution responsibly and make clean handoffs. Long-term portfolio health begins before the merchant ever activates.

What it looks like

  • Pricing and program expectations are clear.
  • The MPA reflects what was actually discussed.
  • Handoffs include the information the next team needs.
  • You remain accountable for the merchant experience.

Ask yourself

"Would I be comfortable explaining this recommendation to the merchant again 6 months from now?"
